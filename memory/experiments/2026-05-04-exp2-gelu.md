# Exp-2: GELU vs ReLU in the feed-forward block

**Date:** 2026-05-05
**Phase:** 2
**Branch / commit:** `feature/sota-upgrade-roadmap` @ `ecb73f6` (pre-experiment); runs produced at this SHA against the Exp-1 default (`attention_scale_mode="head"`).

## Hypothesis

The Phase 1 model uses `nn.ReLU()` between the two FFN Linear layers. GELU (Gaussian Error Linear Units, Hendrycks & Gimpel 2016) is the activation used by GPT-2 and nearly every subsequent LM. Its forward is `x * Φ(x)` (approximated as `0.5 * x * (1 + tanh(...))` in practice), producing a smooth, non-monotonic function that equals ReLU at large positive x and decays gently to 0 at large negative x.

**Prediction before running:** literature suggests 1–3% val_loss reduction on language modeling (B1). Less effect expected on the more structured B2/B3 benchmarks since their attention + FFN stack converges on discrete decisions where activation smoothness matters less.

## Setup

Single flag flip: `ffn_activation="gelu"` (was `"relu"`). The Phase 1 standard contract is held everywhere else; the Exp-1 default (`attention_scale_mode="head"`) stays on since it was promoted to default in commit `ecb73f6`. This means Exp-2 is technically measuring GELU *on top of* the Exp-1-corrected attention scale, not the raw Phase 1 baseline. The comparison here is Exp-2 against the Phase 1 baseline — so both the attention-scale fix and GELU are active in the Exp-2 runs. Individual isolated attribution is impossible without a "GELU-only-no-attn-fix" run; deferred as an open question because the attn-fix delta was small (−0.27% on B1) and unlikely to dominate GELU's expected effect.

## Interpretation

GELU is a substantial win on B1 (−2.83%) and B3 (−3.25%), flat on B2. B1's result matches the top of the predicted 1–3% range. B3's result *exceeds* the prediction, and B2 came in neutral as predicted. Together: two strong wins, one wash.

**Mechanism — B1's language-modeling gain.** GELU's smoothness produces cleaner gradients. ReLU has a hard kink at zero where the derivative jumps from 0 to 1, which introduces a gradient discontinuity — the optimizer learns around the kink rather than through it. Language modeling involves continuous semantic features (embeddings that approximately support arithmetic-like operations), and the FFN block is where those features get transformed. A smoother nonlinearity fits the task better because the target function is itself locally smooth in feature space. This is consistent with Hendrycks & Gimpel's original motivation for GELU and with GPT-2's design choice.

**Mechanism — B3's char-level gain.** The biggest delta of the three, and at first surprising because the prediction said "less effect on structured domains." The explanation ties to the floor effect documented in `memory/findings/2026-05-04-b3-charlevel-floor.md`: B3's char-level SMILES reactions have low intrinsic entropy, so absolute val_loss is small (0.28), and any real quality improvement shows up *larger in relative terms* than on B1. The ReLU-vs-GELU difference is real across all benchmarks; B3's framing just makes it more visible. The "structured domains are less sensitive" intuition is wrong for floor-bounded benchmarks; it's right for noise-dominated ones.

**Mechanism — B2's flat result.** Chess move prediction at 12,341-token ChessTokenizer vocab is neither floor-bounded nor continuous in the same way as language. Attention patterns are discrete (this move token attends to recent move tokens) and FFN transformations produce categorical outputs (this is move-class-X). Activation smoothness doesn't help — the decision boundary is sharp either way.

**VRAM cost.** GELU adds 4–15% peak VRAM vs ReLU. The mechanism: GELU's backward pass needs the pre-activation tensor (derivative of `x * Φ(x)` involves `x` itself), while ReLU backward only needs a boolean mask of which activations were positive. The FFN activation tensor has shape `(B, T, 4*E)` = `(32, 256, 1536) = 12.6M elements` per layer — at fp32 that's 50 MB per layer, ~300 MB for 6 layers. Relative cost scales inversely with total VRAM: B3 (smallest vocab → smallest VRAM baseline → highest relative cost at +15.2%), B1 (largest vocab → lowest relative cost at +4.1%).

**Throughput cost.** ~1% across all three. GELU's extra compute (tanh approximation, a couple of multiplies) vs ReLU's single max. Negligible.

**Prediction calibration note.** I predicted "less effect on structured benchmarks" based on a vague "structure = discrete = activation shape doesn't matter" intuition. This was right for B2 but wrong for B3. The operative distinction isn't "structured vs unstructured," it's "noise-dominated vs floor-bounded." B3 is extremely floor-bounded, so it amplifies real improvements. Updating my mental model: when writing Phase 2+ hypotheses, separate these two dimensions.

## Conclusion

**Ship GELU as default.** Flip `ffn_activation` default from `"relu"` to `"gelu"` in `scratchgpt/config.py`. Justification:

1. Substantial val_loss gain on two of three benchmarks (−2.83% B1, −3.25% B3).
2. B2 neutral (within noise), no regression.
3. Standard practice for every modern LM — keeping ReLU as default in 2026 is anachronistic.
4. Throughput cost (~1%) is negligible.
5. VRAM cost (4–15%) is real but acceptable at this model scale. Phase 3 will benefit from activation checkpointing / bf16 to reclaim most of it.

ReLU remains available via the flag. The plan retains `"relu"` as a valid Literal value for pedagogy and for reproducing Phase 1-era baselines.

## Open questions

- **Isolated attribution of GELU vs. GELU+attention-scale-fix.** The Exp-2 runs had both active. A "GELU-only, keep attention_scale_mode=embedding" run would separate the contributions. Skipping for now — attn-fix's marginal impact on B1 was small (−0.27%), and additive-effect logic suggests GELU's standalone contribution is close to Exp-2's measured delta minus Exp-1's: B1 would be roughly −2.83% − (−0.27%) = −2.56%. Good enough for default-flip decision; multi-seed validation is a better use of future GPU time than retrograde ablation.
- **Does the VRAM cost compound with other Phase 2 flags?** Exp-6 (all-stacked) will tell us.
- **Seed variance.** All Phase 2 runs are single-seed. A −3.25% delta on B3 should be well above seed noise, but it's untested. Tracked for Phase 3's variance-characterization task.
- **GELU approximation choice.** `nn.GELU()` uses the exact Φ formula by default; `nn.GELU(approximate='tanh')` uses the GPT-2 tanh approximation (slightly faster). We're using exact. Phase 4's torch.compile / SDPA work may make this moot.
